# Billing and recovery-control retention evidence

Research date: 2026-09-08. Answers [Billing and recovery-control retention evidence](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/38) for the [MVP map](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/1). **The schedules and protocol changes below are proposals for review, not accepted privacy policy, legal conclusions, deployed configuration, or tested recovery guarantees.**

The accepted inputs are [Authentication and account lifecycle](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/22#issuecomment-5565609337), [Subscription verification and entitlement implementation](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/23#issuecomment-5565993635), [Launch markets and data residency](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/27), and [Backend operating envelope and disaster recovery](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/28#issuecomment-5573796177). Also consume [Local persistence and account synchronization architecture](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/21#issuecomment-5565344184). Workspace context was reviewed in `docs/decisions/` (authentication, subscriptions, synchronization and recovery) and `docs/research/provider-processing-locations.md` and `docs/research/launch-market-privacy-baseline.md`; these workspace files are not bundled into this standalone research branch. The linked issue resolutions are the canonical accepted inputs.

## Finding

Finite cleanup is supportable for private payloads, temporary files, logs, expired credentials, and superseded recovery controls **if every restore and replay path enforces durable current state**. It is not supportable by assigning all receipts a common age. Two limits remain unresolved:

- Public documentation does not establish a final age after which a store purchase can no longer return or identify an earlier owner. Current and terminal ownership indexes need a separately reviewed purpose and unresolved retention endpoint; neither Google's 60-day API window nor Apple's 180-day notification window proves safe ownership erasure.
- Existing random operation IDs and unlimited delayed clients do not alone allow both finite receipt erasure and exact handling of previously unseen versus previously accepted commands. A bounded replay protocol needs explicit design and acceptance, especially for sharing withdrawals. Safe rejection can preserve privacy and accounting but is not proof that all accepted retry behavior remains available.

Neither limit justifies retaining casino sessions, practice details, public prose, emails, or raw receipts indefinitely. The research identifies a proposed schedule, a proof obligation for each compaction, and precise follow-up questions. Independent recovery remains work after MVP functionality; this report implements none of it.

## Provider evidence and its limits

### Authentication and storage

**Supabase Auth.** Sessions last indefinitely by default; refresh tokens do not have an inherent expiry. Access JWTs normally last five minutes to one hour, with one hour the documented default. A JWT contains `session_id`; checking the live session row is necessary to detect a removed session before JWT expiry. Configured session timeouts are evaluated on refresh, and expired rows are cleaned progressively. Consequently, waiting an hour is not a deletion/revocation proof. Keep the accepted live account/session authorization checks on every protected path. A restored refresh-token table must never silently restore revoked authorization. [Supabase sessions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/sessions).

**Supabase backups and exports.** Pro exposes seven days of daily database backups. They exclude Storage object bytes; exports, staging, object versions and other copied files need their own inventory. Project deletion removes associated backups, but deleting an individual account is not project deletion. The documentation describes backup availability, not a contractual maximum for every internal replica, support copy or physical purge. [Database backups](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups). The current public DPA provides return on request within 30 days after agreement expiry and deletion at that period's end; this termination clause does not establish seven-day per-player deletion during service. Verify the signed agreement and per-account operation separately. [Supabase DPA §11.2](https://supabase.com/legal/customer-resources/data-processing-addendum).

**Apple and Google sign-in.** Apple's revocation endpoint needs a valid access or refresh token. Retain the minimum needed token securely until revocation finishes, then erase it; do not erase the only usable evidence first. Apple's technical note gives a manual fallback when tokens are unavailable while still requiring account deletion. That fallback requires qualification against the accepted app lifecycle. [Apple TN3194](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3194-handling-account-deletions-and-revoking-tokens-for-sign-in-with-apple). Google refresh tokens may stop working for several reasons, including six months without use, but active use can keep them alive; that is not a universal maximum. [Google OAuth token expiration](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2#expiration). Provider credential revocation remains distinct from deletion of True Count history.

**Backblaze B2.** Lifecycle rules run once a day after the configured interval; hiding a file is different from deleting its versions. Thus a nominal three-day rule does not prove physical deletion exactly 72 hours after capture. Inventory versions and incomplete multipart uploads as well as visible objects. [B2 lifecycle rules](https://www.backblaze.com/docs/cloud-storage-lifecycle-rules). Object Lock can prevent deletion; compliance retention cannot be shortened and a legal hold has no predetermined expiry. Lock duration, lifecycle rules, copies and keys must agree. [B2 Object Lock](https://www.backblaze.com/docs/cloud-storage-object-lock). US West remains the accepted planned location, not a claim that all provider processing is US-only.

### Billing and delayed ingestion

**Google Play.** A subscription token can query the Developer API until 60 days after expiration. Renewal cancellation is not final expiration. Plan changes can supply `linkedPurchaseToken`. Fully expired out-of-app resubscriptions instead get a new token, with no such link, but may expose prior account identifiers and `expiredPurchaseToken`. Therefore old ownership references can matter after old-token API access ends. New purchases require acknowledgement within three days; ordinary renewals do not. [Google subscription lifecycle](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/subscriptions).

The current `outOfAppPurchaseContext` is available only before acknowledgement. Persist the minimum verified association before RevenueCat or our code acknowledges; qualification must establish which component can observe it first. Capture cannot be postponed indefinitely to solve ownership: preserve the accepted one-hour acknowledgement alert and three-day deadline, represent unresolved activation as pending, and never create another charge or guess a new owner. If RevenueCat acknowledges before the application can obtain indispensable context, return an integration gap. The `itemReplacement` field also has a 60-day availability limit, reinforcing the need to distinguish a provider lookup window from our ownership invariant. [Google SubscriptionPurchaseV2 reference](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/purchases.subscriptionsv2).

**Apple.** Production V2 notification retries occur at successive delays of 1, 12, 24, 48 and 72 hours after the previous attempt: 157 hours in total after the initial attempt. Missed notifications can also be recovered through server APIs. [Apple notification responses](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreservernotifications/responding-to-app-store-server-notifications). Notification history is available for 180 days in production and 30 in sandbox. These are replay windows, not transaction or ownership expiry dates. [Get Notification History](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreserverapi/get-notification-history). Historical notifications describe their original state; use current subscription status for present access. [NotificationHistoryResponse](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreserverapi/notificationhistoryresponse). Original transaction identifiers identify subscription lineage; the checked sources do not promise a maximum lifetime after which all future restore/resubscribe paths lose that relationship. [Apple original transaction identifier](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstorereceipts/original_transaction_id).

**RevenueCat.** Customer deletion removes customer purchase history and can alter financial charts; it does not cancel Apple/Google billing. This is not authority to recreate an app account or transfer benefits. [Customer deletion](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/dashboard-and-metrics/customer-profile#delete-customer). The API supports both completed and queued deletion responses (`200`/`202`); a queued response is not completed purge. Transfers can be filtered by app IDs but are broader than a single selected transaction. [Customer API](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/api-v2/customer). The August 2026 DPA qualifies return/destruction by standard backup and archival practices, without a public maximum covering every end-user copy. Its 30-day termination-request window is not an end-user purge promise. [RevenueCat DPA](https://www.revenuecat.com/dpa). The privacy policy's six-year customer relationship period must not be presented as the retention policy for our players: the policy explicitly distinguishes end-user processing under customer agreements. [RevenueCat privacy policy](https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy-policy).

RevenueCat automatically retries failures five times after 5, 10, 20, 40 and 80 minutes; operators can manually resend. A fresh delivery signature is not a fresh purchase or fresh verification time. [RevenueCat webhooks](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/integrations/webhooks). Deletion/re-ingestion, aliases, retained store-facing identifiers and old customer IDs require sandbox evidence. A provider customer found after our deletion is quarantined for controlled billing reconciliation; it cannot recreate private identity or confer ownership.

**Google Pub/Sub.** Subscription retention defaults to seven days and is configurable from ten minutes to 31 days. Acknowledged messages can be retained for replay. Topic retention, snapshots and dead-letter destinations must also be inventoried; the subscription's 31-day inactivity expiry is a different setting. [Message retention](https://docs.cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/subscription-message-retention). Delivery is not an exactly-once business transaction; consumers must tolerate duplicates. [Subscription overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/subscription-overview). When RevenueCat owns the ingestion configuration, our deployment cannot assume it controls those settings.

### Telemetry and independent store records

**PostHog US.** Person deletion must request deletion of events and, if present, recordings; events are deleted asynchronously and an API exposes completion status. The documented 30-day deletion of a PostHog operator account is not a per-player event SLA. Incoming events after a deletion request need their own prevention. No hard seven-day end-user purge maximum or universal 30-day event TTL was established from the checked page. [PostHog data storage/deletion](https://posthog.com/docs/privacy/data-storage). Keep sensitive bodies out before capture; never send old deletion identities from delayed device telemetry directly without a qualified suppression path. Separate operational metadata from product analytics: the operations decision's 30 days did not resolve every analytics category.

**Other logs.** Supabase exposes component logs with plan-dependent retention. [Supabase logs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/observability/logs). Cloudflare Workers Paid logs currently retain seven days; this is separate from exported copies and other Cloudflare products. [Workers Logs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/workers-logs/). CloudWatch retains logs indefinitely by default; configured expiry normally takes up to another 72 hours to physically delete and can take longer. Set finite retention for the Oregon fallback probe, but do not advertise a hard physical-purge limit unsupported by AWS. [CloudWatch retention](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonCloudWatch/latest/logs/Working-with-log-groups-and-streams.html). The later accepted [Transactional email provider and delivery operations](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/33#issuecomment-5580311915) selects Resend, accepts 30-day provider retention without proactive account-deletion purge, and prohibits independent app copies of message bodies. Treat that as an explicit email-provider retention exception to reconcile with the general seven-day active-provider-copy wording, not as evidence that all processor data disappears in seven days. Suppression-list and internal backup/support retention still need category-specific qualification; recipient-controlled mailboxes are separate.

**Store-controlled records.** Apple describes purchase financial-record retention of at least ten years for most customers, plus purchase-history retention supporting access; this is Apple's purpose and responsibility, not a mandate to retain ten years of True Count account data. [Apple store privacy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/itunes-store/). Google explains that deleting Play History does not remove all purchase/download information. No universal numeric maximum was established for Google's store records. [Google Play history](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/16693200?hl=en). True Count deletion cannot promise deletion of the user's Apple or Google account, store history, or independent exported files.

## Deriving a control horizon

This is a proposed proof and operating target, conditional on the provider qualifications above.

Let **D** be deletion acceptance, **C** the point by which cleanup finishes and every newly produced authorized recovery source embodies the deletion, and **H** the longest verified lifetime of any earlier recoverable state. H includes database snapshots, object versions, raw exports, migration copies, temporary restore environments, independent copies, queues that can restore authoritative state, and retained keys. A copy cannot receive a new retention clock by being copied again. Every copy inherits its source capture time and control watermark.

For the accepted seven-day provider backup and three-day independent-copy design, **H = 7 days only after inventory proves no longer recovery path exists**. Supabase's public documentation alone does not establish all underlying purge limits. The proposed maximum compaction sweep interval is **24 hours**, with deadline alerts; this is a design target to qualify, not permission to extend a listed payload TTL. A daily sweep should begin before each hard expiry where hard expiry is promised.

A superseded control can be removed only when:

1. Every surviving recovery source already contains an equivalent or stronger durable current state, or is permanently excluded and rendered unusable for recovery.
2. Outstanding sensitive operations have a known outcome, or a surviving independent fence prevents their effects.
3. All old credentials, command namespaces and publication permissions are denied by current authorization, including after restore.
4. The replacement control checkpoint is durably protected independently, verified, and discoverable without the failed primary service.

With C no later than D + 7 days, H = 7 days, and a one-day verification/compaction sweep, the **conditional ceiling for active deletion-control retention is D + 15 days**. This is seven days of cleanup plus seven days for the last unsafe source plus one day to verify and compact, not an arbitrary fraud window. Often the control can disappear earlier. If cleanup or a provider purge exceeds its deadline, record a breach of target and a specific unresolved dependency; do not silently reset D or declare completion.

The remaining control row's own backup residue is distinct: if it is removed at D + 15, ordinary seven-day database copies could contain its minimal fields until approximately D + 22, subject to actual provider expiry. Exclude unnecessary control history from ordinary copies where the independently protected checkpoint suffices. Conversely, private payloads cleaned at D + 7 may remain in seven-day backups until approximately D + 14; B2's nominal three-day tail is approximately D + 10 before qualified lifecycle delay. These are derived upper-bound candidates, **not established physical purge SLAs**.

Current ownership, current sharing permission, the live account registry and current allowance state are continuously needed authoritative state, not an expiring audit trail. Their old versions can compact; their current information persists while needed. Rewriting snapshots every three days does not make the underlying information only three days old. Planned B2 three-day backup-file retention can coexist with a longer-lived current control checkpoint only if the control information has its own disclosed schedule. This additional category must be explicitly approved; do not quietly extend all B2 backups.

## Safe compaction and delayed-client reasoning

These are design proofs and required tests, not test results.

### Deny old accounts and sessions without an eternal deletion list

Never reuse account IDs. The authoritative active-account registry creates an account only through new-account enrollment; upload, webhook, provider re-ingestion, restored token and subscription restoration cannot create that row. Every request derives account identity from verified authentication and checks live account/session state at acceptance. Missing accounts and sessions deny access. During recovery, missing state is ambiguous until reconciliation finishes; service remains fenced.

After every pre-deletion recovery source expires or is safely sanitized, absence is a sufficient authorization barrier. For an old device to erase local data, use the separately qualified authenticated lifecycle-status protocol; an expired token, timeout or generic failure is still not deletion proof. A signed client-held deletion receipt may support an initiating device without keeping its server-side contact forever. Exact post-expiry status UX needs review; denial and positive proof of deletion are different capabilities.

Invalidate restored sessions using a nonrollbackable recovery epoch and fresh enrollment, or a qualified equivalent, before reopening. An old refresh token must not silently enroll in that new epoch. Supabase refresh endpoints, RLS, Storage, Realtime, exports and service-role jobs must all respect the barrier. Deleting a blacklist row is safe only because absence and a surviving epoch deny, not because a JWT happens to have expired.

### Records, ordinary retries and feed reset

A proposed server-issued namespace plus per-installation monotonic operation sequence can bound detailed receipts. The server persists a contiguous accepted/disposition floor, not merely the largest sequence seen: holes are unresolved. Keep current item identities/revisions and an account feed floor independently of detailed history. Old namespace requests cannot perform mutations; current requests still compare expected revisions and unique result identity. Compaction installs a coherent snapshot reset and retains local queued changes as proposals.

For an old item ID absent after tombstone compaction, two histories are observationally identical: an offline create that never uploaded, and an accepted item later deleted after the client lost acknowledgement. The server must not classify both as new creates. Preserve the local record and require explicit recovery/review under a new accepted revision/identity as appropriate; preserve source identity for deduplication. A genuine never-uploaded record may require that review too. This is a **proposed reconciliation behavior requiring acceptance**, not an already accepted lossless automatic-sync guarantee. Until qualified, retain the minimal tombstone/identity needed by the existing contract.

Likewise, a missing receipt is not permission to execute again. Safe post-compaction results are current authoritative state, an expired-command/reconciliation result, or a proved original disposition. A new random ID applied to old content is not an acceptable automatic retry. Idempotent result identity and immutable local final result prevent uploading twice from creating two sessions; neither upload can execute payouts. Local gameplay uses its existing atomic sequence/checkpoint/randomness proof, independently of server receipt TTL.

### Sharing withdrawals require more than rejecting old epochs

Suppose device A submits withdrawal W, loses the response, and device B later enables a new sharing period. If W was accepted, replay must not withdraw the new period. If W never arrived, its first acceptance must still withdraw account-wide sharing even though A had an old view. After erasing all evidence distinguishing those histories, the server cannot satisfy both by inspecting W's age or current sharing state.

A proposed per-device contiguous command/disposition floor can distinguish processed W from an unseen sequence, provided command identities are authenticated, holes are accounted for, sequence reuse is rejected and the floor survives backup loss. Exact accepted receipt contents require an outcome representation, not just a high-watermark. A never-seen sequence above that floor can undergo its first account-wide withdrawal; an already accepted W returns its original outcome. Every new enable still checks current generation, and only future eligible sessions enter the new period.

Retiring that device's floor while allowing arbitrary delayed first submissions recreates the ambiguity. Options requiring live review are: retain compact sequence/disposition state for the active account's lifetime; introduce a finite command submission lease followed by explicit user reconciliation for ambiguous old intents; or supply another proof preserving both cases. A finite lease changes delayed-request semantics and cannot be smuggled in as a privacy TTL. Until accepted and qualified, the finite withdrawal-receipt endpoint remains unresolved. Public aggregates and caches must check a current period/generation barrier regardless of receipt history; retired periods never become eligible again.

### Allowance and ownership

A grant's reset identity, device/allocation generation and immutable expiry survive detailed receipt removal through current authoritative floors and protected outcomes. A replay referring to a sealed or expired grant cannot issue a new one. A transferred remainder cannot become available at the frozen source after uncertain acknowledgement. A late completed challenge/result may still upload its preserved history; its allowance/settlement effect stays attached to the original allocation and cannot credit today's budget. If an old unresolved transfer cannot be proved, retain the minimal fence and keep the operation pending; expiring it into a spendable balance is unsafe.

A purchase ownership row is not reconstructed from a webhook's customer ID. Resolve verified lineage and all linked/expired identifiers against the latest protected owner/generation. A previous owner still active means sign in to that owner; deliberate deletion permits only explicit authenticated benefits-only restoration. Competing restores serialize, and stale provider responses cannot advance a newer generation. Raw receipt expiry does not erase this invariant.

For a subscription returning years after a terminal period, validate current store evidence; consult the retained ownership index, including Google's expired purchase/account context captured before acknowledgement. If that index was erased and current evidence cannot prove whether there was an active previous owner, **do not assign the purchase to the requesting account**. Keep activation pending and return a precise integration/retention failure. This preserves safety but may fail the promised restore experience: that unresolved tradeoff belongs in a dedicated protocol decision, not a claim that finite retention is solved. Apple's replay history expiring does not make an old receipt or current resubscription harmless.

## Independent protection before sensitive success

A Postgres transaction and B2 object upload are not one atomic transaction. A daily backup or asynchronously drained transactional outbox leaves a loss interval after acknowledgement. The accepted ordinary-history recovery point permits up to 24 hours of lost history; it does not authorize losing an acknowledged deletion, sharing withdrawal, ownership transfer or spendable allocation.

The eventual control design must establish an authoritative sequence, independent durable outcome and recovery ordering. A possible protocol to qualify is:

1. Serialize and authorize the intent against current account, ownership and permission generations; establish a durable local pending fence. Ordinary deletion/public denial takes effect conservatively while success is still pending.
2. Write a uniquely identified, authenticated minimal intent to an independent control system, with expected predecessor/generation, payload digest and restrictive effect. Obtain a durability acknowledgement. This system must support a qualified single-writer/fencing or compare-and-set discipline and discovery of all accepted records; “object storage” alone does not prove either.
3. Commit the primary decision under the same generation constraints. Persist an independent final outcome/commit certificate and the watermark covering it. For grants/transfers/provider ownership, do not expose a spendable or owned result before the independently durable outcome makes retries and recovery unambiguous.
4. Return sensitive success only when primary enforcement and independent protection are established. Unknown outcomes stay pending/fenced and use the same operation identity on retry. Independent deletion acceptance must itself mean irreversible acceptance, not a speculative intent a recovery job might accidentally apply as confirmed success.
5. On recovery, reconcile intents and final outcomes before opening service. A prepared restriction may conservatively block; a prepared grant cannot authorize spending. Superseding or aborting an operation requires an independently protected decision and cannot undo an accepted deletion. External RevenueCat/Apple/Google side effects need recorded identities, current-state queries and scoped reconciliation; they cannot be rolled back by discarding a database transaction.

The ordering above is an investigation sketch, not a proved distributed commit algorithm. Specify the authority for the decisive acceptance point, fencing across competing writers/restorers, lease expiry, conditional writes, list/read consistency, durable acknowledgement, key recovery, partial external effects and recovery from every step. Test both primary-down and independent-store-down cases. If protection cannot be proved, return pending/unavailable and alert; do not claim success or reopen to meet the 24-hour recovery-time target.

A latest checkpoint must cover a contiguous committed control sequence. Hash chains detect some tampering but cannot alone prove that a suffix is missing; recoverable independent head/watermark metadata is required. Renew checkpoints containing current restrictions before old files expire, then delete superseded files on their original schedules. Missing keys, unknown latest head, a skipped sequence, or unavailable control provider keeps affected operations/public reads fenced. Separate writer, restore and key authority; private-history backup credentials must not also erase the only safety controls.

## Financial and security record qualification

The IRS says ordinary tax supporting records generally last three years after the relevant return, with different rules for refund claims, omitted income, bad debts, unfiled returns and fraud. Those exceptions defeat a universal “all records seven years” rule. A proposed ordinary three-year accounting schedule therefore needs the publisher's filing and entity circumstances checked; any longer case must identify the actual record and reason. [IRS record retention](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records).

Colorado's official published Sales Tax Guide describes a minimum three-year retention for records necessary to determine sales tax. The live guide could not be retrieved in this run; the indexed official August 2021 guide supports only that narrow statement, not a verified conclusion about this publisher's current filing duties or every US marketplace transaction. Confirm the current rule and any home-rule/marketplace obligations with the finance review. [Colorado official guide, Part 9](https://tax.colorado.gov/sites/tax/files/documents/Colorado_Sales_Tax_Guide_August_2021.pdf).

Prefer store settlement reports, payouts, fees, refunds and tax summaries sufficient to support publisher revenue. A player's private casino ledger records their own play, not True Count's sales or taxable payout obligations; simulated training balances are not cash held for players. Retaining those histories as “financial records” is unsupported by this investigation. Any transaction-level reference required for reconciliations should be separated from email, account access, tokens and gameplay. Payment-card retention is unnecessary for the chosen store-only integration.

Colorado's Attorney General identifies duties concerning reasonable security and secure disposal of unneeded personal identifying information. Those duties do not supply a universal 90-day security-log retention minimum. [Colorado data-protection FAQ](https://coag.gov/resources/data-protection-laws/). The broader CPA applicability/rights review remains [US launch privacy rights and release disclosures](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/36); neither a Colorado publisher nor a US-primary region alone settles all state-law applicability. [Colorado CPA overview](https://coag.gov/resources/colorado-privacy-act/).

For actual investigations or legal holds, record authority, categories, minimal identifiers, storage/location, access, start, review date, expected end/release condition and purge owner. A continuing investigation can require a specifically justified extension; a recurring 90-day review cannot disguise indefinite retention. Store-controlled financial records and processor backup constraints must be described separately from app-controlled exceptions.
