# Launch age eligibility: bounded legal evidence

Research date: 2026-09-08. Supports [Launch age eligibility and geographic access](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/34). Assumptions supplied for this investigation: Colorado publisher; US/English distribution; simulated blackjack, craps and roulette training; community strategy publication and ratings; subscriptions and advertising; no real-money wagering or prizes. This is decision support, not a compliance certification or a fifty-state survey. No audience decision is recorded here.

## Federal and Colorado baseline

COPPA addresses personal information from children under 13, including general-audience services with actual knowledge. It does not impose a universal 18+ or 21+ access rule. FTC staff says a general-audience operator need not investigate every visitor's age; a service choosing to screen should use a neutral mechanism that permits truthful underage responses. A bare checkbox declaring adulthood should not be described as FTC-approved neutral screening: the FAQ expressly rejects its analogous over-12 checkbox. Later reliable notice that a user is under 13 matters despite an earlier adult declaration. Terms excluding children do not alone establish that a service is general audience. [FTC COPPA FAQ, especially A.12 and H](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).

The current rule covers passive identifiers and public posting as well as account forms; the revised text includes biometric and government-issued identifiers. Operational review must account for SDK collection before registration and information made public through community features. Use the current rule rather than assuming all older FAQ wording reflects the 2025 amendments. [16 CFR Part 312](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312).

On February 25, 2026 the FTC announced conditional enforcement discretion for certain general- and mixed-audience services collecting personal information solely for age verification without prior parental consent. Conditions include purpose limitation, prompt deletion, security, restricted recipients and notice. This is not a requirement to collect government ID or a blanket exception allowing reuse of verification information. [FTC announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/02/ftc-issues-coppa-policy-statement-incentivize-use-age-verification-technologies-protect-children).

Colorado SB24-041 took effect October 1, 2025. Its special minor-data provisions apply regardless of processing volume or revenue, when the controller conducts business in Colorado or targets Colorado residents. They address services offered to consumers the controller knows or willfully disregards are minors, require reasonable care regarding heightened harm, and impose assessment and consent duties in specified circumstances. Targeted advertising, sale, certain profiling, retention, geolocation and engagement features are among the regulated uses. The act expressly does not require affirmative age verification or age gating. Thus small launch scale is not a universal exemption, and an adult-only policy does not authorize ignoring credible minor information. [Enacted bill summary](https://www.leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB24-041).

Colorado's gaming regulator describes a 21-year minimum for participation in Colorado casino gambling. That does not establish a 21-year legal minimum for this assumed no-prize training product. Treat 18+ versus 21+ as an audience/product-policy decision until the actual mechanics, advertising and distribution are assessed. The bounded scan found no primary source imposing a blanket 21+ rule on these assumptions; this is not a finding that every state permits every simulation design. [Colorado gaming regulator fact book, age questions](https://sbg.colorado.gov/sites/sbg/files/documents/DOG_CY2019%20Fact%20Book%20and%20Abstract%20Final.pdf).

Texas sections 121.051–.056 cover developers distributing through app stores in the state. Duties include app and purchase age ratings, significant-change notices, and a system using store information to verify age category and minor consent. Store-supplied personal data has restricted purposes and must be deleted when verification completes; it should not become an indefinitely retained raw audit record. The statutory adult category is 18+, so it alone does not prove 21+. [Texas Chapter 121](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.121.v3.htm). The current status above is supported by [Google's current implementation guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691?hl=en) and the [Supreme Court docket, 25A1390](https://www.supremecourt.gov/search.aspx?filename=/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25a1390.html).

Utah's amended developer requirements include requesting store age/consent information and notifying stores of significant changes, beginning May 6, 2027. [Current Utah section 13-76-202](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title13/Chapter76/C13-76-S202_2026031820260318.pdf).

Louisiana's replacement requires store-based category/consent checks, enforcement of developer-created age restrictions, and significant-change notices. It specifies handling for conflicts between store and internal signals; it does not simply retain the old law's lowest-age wording. Sections 1, 5 and 6 establish the repeal and commencement distinction. [Act185 signed text, especially pages 6–8 and 11](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1475238), [bill history](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/BillInfo.aspx?i=251085).

California AB1043 requires developers to request signals and treats a received signal as actual knowledge across application access points, subject to stronger conflicting information. Sharing and data requests are limited. [Enacted AB1043](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB1043). AB1856 is an amendment to monitor; the official history available on the research date shows enrollment and presentation to the Governor, not a basis here to treat its proposed changes as already enacted. [AB1856 history](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billHistoryClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB1856).

Colorado's enacted 2026 law requires covered applications to request an age signal at initial launch or first account creation when operative; it limits additional information and sharing and addresses conflicting age information. Its final July 2028 date supersedes earlier fiscal-note schedules. [SB26-051 enacted summary and session-law date](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-051).

## Decisions and remaining uncertainty

Apple explicitly says even 18+-rated apps must use its Declared Age Range API in legally required regions. Store content ratings, permission to download, product eligibility and geographic access are separate. Older supported operating systems also need a deliberate path because signal availability differs. [Apple age assurance Q&A](https://developer.apple.com/support/age-assurance).

Engineering implications proposed for review, not statutory quotations:

- An 18+ policy fits the common statutory adult bracket. A 21+ policy needs a separate way to distinguish 18–20 from 21+, because an adult signal can be insufficient.
- Prefer minimum useful age information and required platform signals over default collection of exact DOB or identity documents. Do not declare a single universal checkbox sufficient for every US native-app user.
- Decide explicitly how underage, unknown, refused, unavailable and conflicting signals affect access. If there is no minor experience, credible underage information should stop ordinary product access and initiate the appropriate account/data handling; parental store consent need not override the product's chosen minimum.
- Keep unknown eligibility distinct from confirmed adulthood. A platform-unavailable path may differ from a failed mandatory regional check; the actual platform APIs and supported OS versions must determine that design.
- Keep private histories and age evidence out of advertising, public profiles and community payloads. Define finite retention for any derived eligibility record after reviewing state restrictions on source-data retention.

This scan does not settle whether the particular community design meets any state's social-media definition, current injunctions affecting those laws, advertising-provider restrictions, all gambling/virtual-currency classifications, or travel/residency enforcement. Reassess if prizes, transferable value, purchased wagering credits, gambling affiliate advertising, open messaging, feeds or youth-directed marketing enter scope. Native-app store duties do not by themselves answer the separate browser-access policy.
