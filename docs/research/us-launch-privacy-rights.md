# US launch privacy rights

Checked: 2026-09-11. Supports issue #36. Assumptions: Colorado publisher, US-only English launch, users 13+, private casino/practice records, contextual ads, approximately 1,000 MAU growing to 5,000. This is a bounded primary-source review, not a fifty-state certification. Subscription forfeiture remains issue #45.

## Findings that matter at small scale

Monthly active users do not establish annual unique-consumer counts, revenue, data-sale revenue share, affiliates or statutory coverage. Colorado's general thresholds are 100,000 Colorado consumers annually or 25,000 with revenue/discounts from data sales. California additionally has revenue and sale/sharing-revenue-share tests. Assess those facts; do not claim that 5,000 MAU exempts the app from privacy law. [Colorado AG](https://coag.gov/resources/colorado-privacy-act/), [CPPA applicability](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq).

CalOPPA independently requires a conspicuous privacy policy for covered commercial services collecting California consumers' information. Include categories collected/recipients, review or change process if provided, material-change notification, effective date, tracking-signal response and whether third parties track across services. It has no CCPA revenue threshold. Its tracking disclosure duty is not itself a blanket requirement to honor every browser DNT signal. [BPC 22575](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22575.).

The FTC requires app developers to honor express and implied privacy promises. A voluntary nationwide rights promise becomes an operational obligation; do not publish deadlines or “we never share data” claims that provider behavior contradicts. [FTC app guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/marketing-your-mobile-app-get-it-right-start).

Colorado separately requires reasonable security, written disposal policies for covered identifying information and service-provider safeguards. Breach notification can be required without CPA-scale coverage: generally without unreasonable delay and within 30 days of determining a qualifying breach, with statutory exceptions; AG notice applies at 500 affected Coloradans. Classify the actual records: a practice balance is not automatically a financial-account credential. [Colorado data-protection guidance](https://coag.gov/resources/data-protection-laws/).

Nevada's online privacy law separately requires a notice and designated no-sale request address for covered operators. Its narrow small-site notice exception includes being located in Nevada, so Colorado location does not satisfy it. The ordinary response ceiling is 60 days, with a explained necessary extension of up to 30 days. [NRS 603A.330–.345](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-603a.html).

Texas's general SBA-small-business exemption has an exception: sensitive-data sales require prior consent under section 541.107. Small size is not authorization to sell sensitive data. [Texas Chapter 541](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.541.htm).

## Teen-specific requirements

Colorado SB24-041's special minor provisions took effect October 1, 2025 without the general volume/revenue thresholds. Known or willfully disregarded under-18 users trigger reasonable care and specified consent duties. Consent is required for targeted advertising, sales, significant-decision profiling, incompatible purposes, retention beyond reasonable service necessity and engagement-extending design features. Heightened-risk processing requires an assessment; document its conclusions and retention. The law does not impose universal affirmative age verification. Indefinite retention of closed teen accounts therefore needs a defensible requested-service purpose or appropriate consent; it is not automatically safe because the account is “soft closed.” The threshold bypass covers specified sections, not automatically every general CPA consumer-rights provision. [Enacted summary](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-041), [signed act, sections 3–6](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2024a_041_signed.pdf).

New York's Child Data Protection Act took effect June 20, 2025. For covered 13–17-year-olds, processing needs informed user consent unless strictly necessary for an enumerated purpose. Internal operations expressly exclude marketing/advertising; the AG identifies a narrower fraud-prevention example involving ad frequency capping. Therefore **contextual-only ads are not automatically consent-free**: inspect actual AdMob processing rather than equating nonpersonalized selection with no personal-data use. [NY AG implementation guidance](https://ag.ny.gov/child-data-protection-act-guidance).

New York consent must be separately informed and revocable; refusal cannot cause withholding, degradation or increased pricing. It prohibits purchasing/selling minor data, limits consent re-requests and recognizes relevant device signals. These requirements have no general MAU/revenue threshold in their coverage definition. A no-ad fallback while retaining the ordinary free service fits the launch's existing fallback policy, subject to SDK qualification. [GBL 899-EE](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-EE), [GBL 899-FF](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-FF).

California separately gives registered known minors a right to remove or request removal of their posted content, with notice and clear instructions. This concerns material accessible by others, not only private account records. Removal need not erase third-party copies; the statute permits anonymization and enumerated exceptions. Any blanket promise to retain community contributions permanently must account for this. [BPC 22581](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22581.). Known-minor advertising also has restricted product categories, including alcohol, tobacco, cannabis and lottery tickets; contextual selection alone does not establish compliance with every subsection. [BPC 22580](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22580.).

COPPA remains relevant if the service is child-directed or obtains actual knowledge of an under-13 user. A 13+ term does not defeat those facts; preserve the established eligibility and underage handling. [FTC COPPA guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).

## Rights and timing

Covered Colorado users have access, correction, deletion, portability and opt-out rights; ordinary responses are due within 45 days, with an explained necessary extension. Covered CCPA requests to know/correct/delete require acknowledgment within 10 business days and substantive response within 45 calendar days; sale/sharing and sensitive-use opt-outs have a maximum 15-business-day compliance period. Those are ceilings, not reasons to defer an available automated action. [Colorado AG](https://coag.gov/resources/colorado-privacy-act/), [CPPA deadlines](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq).

Colorado recognizes GPC as a universal opt-out mechanism. CCPA-covered sale/sharing must likewise honor qualifying signals. GPC concerns sale/targeted advertising, not account erasure or an automatic opt-out of all contextual advertising. Even if qualification concludes there is no covered sale/targeted processing, implement and explain signal handling truthfully rather than inventing such activity. [Colorado recognized mechanisms](https://coag.gov/opt-out/), [CPPA signal guidance](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq).

An optional 30-day undo period plus seven-day cleanup is 37 days from the original request. Do not reset a statutory request clock at day 30, or treat undo as mandatory. Offer immediate irreversible deletion; distinguish immediate access revocation from seven-day active-system cleanup and separately disclosed backup expiry. A 30-day request response promise must explain scheduled deletion rather than falsely claiming complete erasure on day 30. No source here establishes a statutory financial-retention period for casino logs, practice records or entire billing payloads.

## Proposed simple nationwide contract — product proposal

Offer every US user the same baseline, without requiring proof of state residency:

- Access and download personal records in a usable format; correct inaccuracies; delete selected records or erase the account; withdraw optional-processing consent; request restrictions on sale, targeted advertising and unnecessary sensitive-data uses; appeal a denied request.
- Accept requests in app and through a public web privacy/help route, including for people who cannot sign in. Use proportionate identity verification for disclosure/deletion; provide a recovery/support path without default government-ID collection. Do not require authentication merely to accept GPC or an ordinary advertising opt-out.
- Acknowledge promptly, within 10 business days; respond substantively within 30 calendar days. Review appeals within 30 calendar days, explain refusals/retained categories and give the relevant regulator complaint route. These are proposed service commitments, not claims that every state mandates identical rights or deadlines.
- Apply optional-processing withdrawal and opt-outs immediately where technically possible, including GPC on browser surfaces. Disable the relevant SDK processing before consent; if consent is missing, refused or unqualified, use the existing no-ad fallback without reducing the free service.
- Preserve optional 30-day deletion undo and an equally accessible immediate option. Revoke access when irreversible deletion begins and finish active cleanup within seven days; show the scheduled date and exceptions honestly. Complete ordinary statutory requests on their original clocks.
- Provide minor contribution removal and an accessible, dated privacy notice covering real SDK recipients, purposes, retention, rights, public-content limitations and policy changes. Record narrow retention exceptions by actual legal/operational purpose and finite schedule; no blanket “financial law requires keeping everything” claim.

The existing community contract distinguishes voluntary unpublishing from required removal following a validated legal complaint. Minor privacy removal can use that existing support/report exception without requiring whole-account deletion; ordinary delisting alone must not be presented as statutory removal. BPC 22581 excludes certain third-party copies/reposts, so this note does not propose erasing all independent remixes automatically.

The proposal simplifies the interface. It does not replace provider contracts, teen risk/consent qualification, incident response, retention engineering or rechecking applicable state coverage as the audience and features grow.
