# Local persistence and account synchronization — accepted decision

Accepted through the live Q1–Q15 discussion, including the integrated design and explicit follow-ups. The resolution on [Local persistence and account synchronization architecture](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/21#issuecomment-5565344184), part of the [MVP decision map](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/1), is canonical; this file preserves the reviewed decision for repository readers. This is a planning resolution, not implemented software or proof that native acceptance checks have passed.

## Accepted in this session

- **Q1 — Hosting:** use a managed backend/database service; application synchronization rules remain our responsibility.
- **Q2 — Budget:** target $50/month initially for database, API, storage, and backups, separately from the accepted simulation-compute budget. Provider costs and workload must inform selection.
- **Q4 — Local persistence:** use SQLite for saved activities, accounting, and pending uploads. Commit game transitions and accounting together before displaying their outcomes, with one coordinated writer and durable settings. Native crash-recovery verification remains required.
- **Q5 — Backend provider:** use Supabase. The user accepted it after comparing Neon, Cloudflare D1, Firebase, and Convex, with existing Supabase familiarity weighing against speculative hosting savings. Plan around the $25/month Pro starting cost within the $50/month backend target, subject to actual usage and operational costs. Detailed authentication methods and subscription verification remain their existing tickets. Disaster recovery remains deferred; selecting Supabase does not accept the additional point-in-time recovery cost or establish the provisional recovery targets.
- **Q6 — Private records:** the backend may process private histories for synchronization and account totals. Use encryption in transit and at rest, account-level access controls, tightly restricted operational access, and exclude private record contents from analytics and routine logs. Casino sessions remain strictly private. Operator-unreadable end-to-end encryption is not the MVP design.
- **Q7 — Device account isolation:** use a separate encrypted SQLite database for each account, with its key in device secure storage. Ordinary sign-out closes account access while retaining its unsynced records and key. Signing back into the same account restores access under the accepted online sign-in requirement after explicit sign-out. Reinstallation restores synced records through online sign-in; recovery of device-only records is not guaranteed. SQLCipher/SecureStore integration requires native verification.
- **Q8 — Synchronization timing:** upload after saving; reconcile on app opening and reconnection. While open and connected, private Supabase notifications trigger fetching changes, with periodic reconciliation to catch missed notifications. Background synchronization is best effort under operating-system scheduling. Show pending/error status. The integrated design below specifies retry, polling, notification authorization and ordering contracts.
- **Q9 — History download:** on a new device, obtain all session summaries and lifetime totals, plus the latest 90 days of detailed history initially. Download older details when opened and offer Download all history for offline use. Preserve records already created or downloaded locally; never remove unfinished activities or unsynced records to save space. Detailed records absent locally require connectivity until downloaded. History download and totals must preserve the previously accepted revision/conflict baseline rules.
- **Q10 — Trusted offline time:** include small native platform adapters to preserve trustworthy elapsed time across ordinary app restarts where continuity is established. Reboot, missing evidence or inconsistent readings require reconnection for time-limited access; permitted completion, settlement and history remain available. Qualify the proposed iOS mechanism on devices before promising restart continuity.
- **Q11 — Connectivity uncertainty:** validate usable internet independently of Supabase with lightweight HTTPS probes on separate infrastructure. Supabase failure alone does not stop an already-authorized foreground activity. When connectivity is uncertain, apply the accepted offline stopping rules: finish the current turn and permitted settlement, while preserving the started-challenge exception. Explain uncertainty rather than falsely asserting the device is offline.
- **Q12 — Older synchronization protocols:** an incompatible protocol may require an app update. Preserve unsynced records, local history and permitted recovery; the update must migrate them correctly even across skipped releases. Give at least 30 days' notice before normal protocol retirement; urgent security defects may require earlier restriction. A required update never justifies discarding records.
- **Q13 — Individual synchronization failures:** retain and flag a repeatedly failing record; pause dependent operations and continue independent synchronization. Pending privacy withdrawals block affected public contributions, and unresolved allowance reconciliation blocks the corresponding transfer/grant. Never automatically delete failed work or retry in a rapid endless loop.
- **Q14 — Timed allowance after abrupt termination:** charge only durably recorded active time. Checkpoint approximately every second while healthy and alongside game actions. After an abrupt termination, retain committed usage and discard the unrecorded tail; never charge the interval until the next launch. A small amount of practice may remain uncharged, and stalls prevent a guaranteed one-second bound. Detected persistence failure pauses further training.

## Follow-up decisions

[Backend operating envelope and disaster recovery](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/28) owns backup selection, recovery operations, retention, and restore validation. Restoring older data must also address withdrawn sharing, deleted records, command receipts, and allowance grants; restoring the database is not by itself proof of correct service recovery.
- Monthly active players, sessions per player, persisted result sizes, and synchronization traffic have not been established. The same operations follow-up owns these workload and cost assumptions. Any pricing examples must identify their assumptions rather than present them as forecasts.
- [Launch markets and data residency](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/27) owns target markets, production region selection and associated provider/residency requirements.

## Existing contracts

Preserve the accepted [offline lifecycle](offline-access-subscription-lifecycle.md) and [mobile/shared-engine architecture](mobile-platform-shared-game-engine.md). In particular: device-local unfinished activity, atomic durable transitions before reveal, stable retry identities, server-accepted conflict baselines, account-wide sharing-period withdrawal, one allowance device, no duplicated grants or settlement effects, and skipped-version recovery. ## Evidence

- [SQLite synchronous settings](https://www.sqlite.org/pragma.html#pragma_synchronous) distinguish WAL NORMAL from FULL durability. Proposed configuration details and actual device behavior still require implementation verification.
- Backend price and capability comparison: [research findings](../research/backend-provider-costs.md). The comparison informed the accepted Supabase choice; whole-stack costs still depend on workload and operational requirements.

## Implementation design — accepted in Q15

The following makes the accepted choices concrete. The user accepted this integrated design in Q15. It is a planning design and acceptance contract, not implemented software or evidence of passing native tests.

### Storage and ownership

Use Expo SQLite with SQLCipher, one database and random encryption key per account and installation, and Expo SecureStore for the key. Use WAL and explicitly set and verify `synchronous=FULL` on every writing connection. Serialize writes through one account storage controller; use the exclusive transaction object for every statement inside a transition. Background synchronization and foreground play share that writer. The portable engine only computes state transitions and never owns storage, account access, or network calls.

Keep authoritative checkpoint data, command receipts, wager/settlement facts, funding and allowance effects, assessment evidence, pinned definitions, and the pending-upload queue in the same account database. Pinned definitions needed to recover an activity must be retained locally; deletion of a downloaded source must not remove definitions still referenced by an unfinished activity. Store exact numeric values using the engine's canonical integer/rational encoding, not floating-point database columns. Validate versions, lengths and hashes when reading stored artifacts. Presentation caches are reconstructible; authoritative activity state is not.

Use device-only key accessibility where supported. Ordinary sign-out retains keys and databases but closes handles, removes views from memory, cancels account requests/subscriptions, and increments a local account-session generation. Every asynchronous completion checks that generation and account before applying data or opening a database. Token expiry or a failed renewal request does not silently sign the user out or erase cached rights. Confirmed account revocation/deletion follows the account-lifecycle decision rather than being treated as a transient error. Explicit sign-out requires successful online sign-in to that same account before reopening its data.

Exclude live databases, their WAL/sidecar files, access anchors, installation identifiers and key material from OS backup/device-transfer paths; test the actual native backup configuration. A new installation must register online and cannot infer allowance ownership from restored files or surviving iOS keychain entries. Retain unsynced data on normal sign-out. Distinguish a temporarily inaccessible key (for example a locked device) from permanent loss; never replace an unreadable database with an empty one. Key loss/corruption produces an explained recovery state, preserving the original bytes while synced history is recovered where possible. Account export/deletion and remote revocation semantics remain with the account-lifecycle ticket.

Expo documents [SQLCipher](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/sqlite/#sqlcipher) and [SecureStore lifecycle/backup behavior](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/securestore/). Their availability does not prove account isolation or migration correctness for this application.

### Atomic execution, completion and migration

Create the activity identity, initial randomness and pinned execution specification durably before first content. For every command, atomically compare the expected activity sequence and commit its receipt, next checkpoint, generated outcomes, settlement/accounting effects, pending reveal/action phase and assessment changes. Only then reveal outcomes or accept another input. A repeated command returns its existing result; reuse of an identity with different content is an error. A stale sequence cannot advance the activity.

When acknowledgement is uncertain, reopen/read the committed receipt before attempting any new transition. A retry before commit recomputes from unchanged random state; a retry after commit returns the existing transition. Persist scored illegal attempts and correction state without changing wagers. Final settlement uses the same protocol, credits only original funding, admits no new wagers, and consumes no additional training allowance. Completion writes one immutable result and one upload operation in the same transaction. Uploading a result never reapplies its local payouts or charges.

Do not depend on a background/termination callback to save already-shown progress. Disk-full, locked-key and write failures pause progression before revealing another uncommitted state. Keep the last good committed checkpoint and display save/retry status. If recovery is truly unusable, retain recoverable history and mark the activity incomplete; do not manufacture outcomes, personal bests or readiness milestones.

Quiesce all writers before migration. Take a consistent encrypted SQLite backup through a supported backup mechanism that includes committed WAL state, and verify it before starting a migration. Migrate transactionally or use a staged database replacement with a durable manifest that identifies the committed generation. Do not run gameplay while two generations could be active. Restart must recover either the unchanged old generation or the complete migrated generation; it must never restore a pre-gameplay backup over newer committed play.

Test direct upgrades from every persisted schema/semantics family that supported installs may still hold, including skipped releases and each unfinished phase. Preserve old executable semantics where an equivalent migration cannot be demonstrated. Keep schema, engine/rules, strategy instructions, teaching references and random-protocol versions distinct. A rollback that cannot read the current schema is blocked rather than writing older-format data. Normal server protocol retirement gives 30 days' notice; urgent restrictions remain explained. Retained local work migrates through the supported update path before synchronization resumes.

### Supabase transaction boundary and synchronization feed

Use versioned database operations exposed through the Supabase API for private synchronization and account-state mutations. Each logical acceptance runs inside one Postgres transaction; do not spread revision acceptance, accounting and operation receipts across separate HTTP calls. External subscription/provider calls occur outside that transaction; their verified results enter through a dedicated authorized operation owned by the subscription service.

Derive the account from verified authentication, never from a caller-supplied owner field. Restrict table grants and use row-level security on exposed tables. Clients cannot directly write accepted balances, grants, sharing state, revision baselines, receipts or feed positions. Privileged functions require explicit account authorization, fixed object lookup paths and narrowly granted execution; service credentials stay on the server. [Supabase database functions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/functions), [Row-level security](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security).

Give each account a synchronization-head row. All mutations that affect its authoritative private/account state acquire this row before allocating a new account sequence, then commit state changes, receipt and feed entries together. This serializes sequence allocation with commit order: a later-committing lower sequence cannot hide behind a device's already-advanced cursor. Use a consistent lock order for any additional rows and bounded transaction retries. Server jobs affecting the same state obey the same boundary.

An upload operation carries a stable random operation ID, protocol version, originating installation, item identity, expected accepted revision where relevant, and a canonical request hash. A durable receipt uniquely identifies the account/operation and its accepted or conflicting disposition. Retrying identical content returns that disposition; differing content under the same ID is rejected. Persist a receipt and content-change feed independently of transient HTTP acknowledgement. Retain compact deduplication identities for as long as the account/record can accept delayed uploads; normal retry-age expiry cannot recreate a deleted item or duplicate a grant. Exact account-deletion retention is owned by its existing lifecycle decision.

The local upload queue stores payloads, dependencies and receipt status durably with the local changes. Preserve order for edits to one item, and map an acknowledged local predecessor to its server revision before sending its successor. Use a new operation identity only for a genuinely new or explicitly resolved edit. Batch independent private records where practical, but isolate validation failures so one item does not roll back unrelated accepted work.

Long histories and deviation logs may exceed one request or database row. Split immutable detail into bounded content-hashed chunks staged privately in Postgres, with stable owner/result/chunk identities. Keep the local manifest and chunk references with the completion operation. Final server acceptance verifies the complete manifest and available chunks before atomically making the result, summary, progress evidence and feed entry visible. Never publish a partial result or count staged chunks as another completed session. The client retains its local data until final receipt; an expired abandoned staging upload can resend the same bytes without creating another result. Choose and test a conservative request/chunk limit against the pinned provider configuration; an oversized result cannot justify truncating history or assessment evidence.

Reconnect first authenticates the remembered account as needed and reconciles compatibility, access, allowance ownership and queued privacy withdrawals. Accept new grants or public contributions only after their required reconciliation. Private history can continue independently of an unrelated failed record. Upload completion applies the server disposition and queue acknowledgement atomically on the device; a lost response merely leaves an operation pending for an identical retry.

Fetch changes after the durable per-account cursor, in bounded pages. Apply each page and its new cursor atomically. Historical bootstrap uses a bounded, consistent server snapshot with its corresponding watermark; subsequent deltas start there. Persist download progress, do not advance over unapplied changes, and show incomplete-bootstrap status. Any feed compaction must support an explicit snapshot reset while preserving the local pending queue and conflict proposals. Never silently skip an expired cursor.

For the initial implementation, materialize the private summary snapshot, matching totals and watermark in one server transaction using the account synchronization-head lock. Page immutable snapshot rows afterward, rather than assuming separate HTTP requests share a database snapshot. Do not copy all historical detail into this snapshot. Expire temporary snapshot rows after 24 hours; a client that has not finished obtains a fresh snapshot while preserving its local queue. Stage incoming bootstrap pages locally, then install the coherent baseline and cursor together before applying later feed entries. Existing offline proposals remain separate throughout. Include snapshot rows and staged uploads in account-deletion cleanup. Size-test snapshot creation so large histories do not hold an account lock for an unacceptable period; an optimized replacement must preserve the same observable contract.

### Concurrent edits, deletion and totals

A casino session or saved private strategy has one server-accepted revision. An edit names the revision it was based on. If it matches and the item has no unresolved conflict, accept it atomically. A stale competing edit becomes a preserved proposal and freezes the current accepted revision as the conflict baseline. Further devices add proposals; their acknowledgements or displayed clocks cannot replace the baseline. Independent new sessions merge by identity.

Resolution requires connectivity and identifies the current conflict generation and reviewed proposals. If another proposal arrives before resolution commits, return the changed conflict for review rather than discard an unseen edit. An accepted resolution establishes one new revision. Remove rejected temporary proposal payloads after the decision is durably represented; keep only the minimum receipt/identity information needed to prevent retries resurrecting them. This is reconciliation, not a permanent casino correction audit trail.

A server-accepted deletion is a tombstone with zero contribution to totals. A later offline edit is a recovery proposal, never automatic restoration. If a deletion itself is based on an obsolete revision, preserve it as a competing proposal under the same rule; do not silently erase an intervening accepted edit. Explicit recovery produces a new accepted revision only after review.

Canonical lifetime totals count one accepted revision per casino session, including a frozen conflict baseline, and exclude deleted items. Adjust totals by removing the prior accepted contribution and adding the new one within the acceptance transaction. Keep the accepted exact money encoding and currency boundary. Private practice results and casino totals remain separate. An unsynced new record is labeled local; a pending edit does not pretend to be an accepted account total.

On a new device, summary bootstrap and lifetime aggregates must describe the same server watermark. Apply summary deltas and matching totals consistently. Fetch the newest 90 days of details after summaries; older detail is retrieved by its accepted revision, and a changed revision is reconciled before display. Provide resumable Download all history. Keep locally created/downloaded records and immutable references; do not prune unsynced, unfinished or previously retained history silently. If space is insufficient, stop additional downloads and explain the storage problem without evicting protected data.

Merge completed challenge results and eligible personal bests only within the accepted matching configuration/reference and Learn/Check/assistance groups. Preserve interruption flags and prohibit incomplete attempts from earning bests or readiness credit. Give attempts a durable originating-device sequence, including abandoned/incomplete attempts, and retain each device's separate streak evidence. Late uploads fill their original positions; do not infer order from displayed clocks, stitch devices into a streak, or grant a streak-based milestone across a missing attempt. Earned valid milestones synchronize as account history. The challenge decision remains the source of scoring and streak-breaking rules.

### Sharing withdrawal and public contributions

Keep private result upload separate from public contribution acceptance. A public contribution carries its result identity, immutable strategy revision and the sharing permission/period captured at the practice session's start. Enabling sharing midway through a session cannot make that existing session eligible; only future sessions qualify. The server checks current permission, account/profile eligibility and unchanged period. Result upload alone cannot enable sharing or backfill an old period. Casino sessions never enter this path.

Queue offline withdrawal durably and stop sharing locally immediately. Server acceptance invalidates the affected sharing period and excludes all covered practice/popularity contributions, including other devices' intervening uploads, while retaining private records. Concurrent contribution acceptance and withdrawal serialize against the account state. A contribution accepted first is covered by withdrawal; one arriving later with a revoked period is rejected from public use.

On first acceptance, a new account-wide withdrawal disables the server-current sharing state and covers existing practice/popularity contributions even if the sending device had an older view. A concurrent enable request names its expected permission generation and must retry after a changed generation. By contrast, redelivery of the same already-accepted withdrawal operation returns its stored receipt without applying withdrawal again. Server serialization, not a stale client timestamp, distinguishes a new withdrawal from a retry.

Public reads and cached aggregate versions must respect the withdrawal barrier before reporting removal confirmed. Background physical cleanup may follow, but stale aggregate/cache data must no longer be served as current. Re-enabling sharing creates a new period for future eligible sessions only. Explicit ratings retain their separate withdrawal behavior. Retries of an already-processed withdrawal return its original receipt and cannot disable a later sharing period anew. An offline withdrawal that has not yet reached the server remains visibly pending; do not fabricate a timestamp-based earlier enforcement point.

### Allowance ownership, transfers, policy and reset

Store the authoritative allowance device, ownership generation, policy assignment, scheduled reset boundaries and grant identities on the server. One grant identity per account/server reset sequence prevents duplicate issuance; a client-local calendar date is not the uniqueness key. A grant states its original device/ownership generation, immutable policy version, guided-practice method and amounts, separate challenge budget, start and expiry. Its device commits the grant before spending it. Server responses to the same issuance request return the same grant; reconnect/reinstall/upgrade cannot refill it.

Record local usage as ordered uniquely identified effects linked to that grant and the originating activity. Reconciliation deduplicates effects and verifies ownership, policy, amounts and sequence completeness. It does not claim that an offline client is tamper-proof. New-day grants remain possible on the current allowance device once required usage/ownership reconciliation succeeds. Old final-settlement effects remain attached to the expired grant/session and never change the new budget. An outstanding settlement must finish before that device starts conflicting funded play or transfers its remaining balance.

For transfer, first durably freeze new source-device allowance spending, finish guided practice/final settlement and reconcile the source's complete usage sequence. The server then atomically seals the source allocation and assigns exactly the remainder to the target under a new ownership/allocation generation. The target durably commits the accepted allocation before use. Lost acknowledgement leaves the source frozen until it retrieves the transfer receipt; it cannot assume failure and reopen the same budget. Cancellation or reversal requires a new authoritative operation proving where the balance resides. An already-charged challenge may stay on its original device and finish without another attempt charge.

Lost-device replacement changes future ownership immediately but permits the replacement's first grant only at the next scheduled reset. The old device's existing offline allocation expires at its unchanged boundary; it cannot obtain future grants. If the original device returns before expiry, reconcile and perform the explicit remainder-transfer protocol. Missing data never justifies granting the same remainder again. Reinstallation registers a new installation and follows replacement/transfer rules rather than replaying restored allowance files.

Use server-maintained IANA timezone rules and persist exact UTC grant boundaries. A timezone change preserves the current expiry and schedules the first new-zone reset at its next midnight strictly after that expiry. No grant fills an intervening gap; preview the exact next reset before confirmation. Serialize competing timezone changes with grant issuance. A repeated timezone edit cannot shorten the previously protected boundary or create a second budget.

Assign experiments/policies on the server with stable account membership. Deliver immutable policy versions, their notice state and reset-based effective time. A returning player who missed notice keeps the prior assigned policy for the current allowance day and receives the new version at the next eligible reset. Record notice acknowledgement as account state without treating a failed request as successful. Policies, consumption and historical results remain linked; premium transitions never refund or duplicate a daily allowance.

### Timed allowance after abrupt termination — Q14

Measure active-time segments using native elapsed readings, separately from deadline verification and challenge performance timing. Close segments on observed pause/background/correction/setup/results transitions, and persist accumulated usage with gameplay writes and periodic timer checkpoints. The OS cannot generally provide the exact termination instant after an abrupt kill.

Accepted rule: retain only durably observed active time after an unclean termination; never charge the interval until the next launch. Target a timer checkpoint every second while healthy and before accepting another game action, and flush on observed normal pause/background events. A crash can leave a small uncharged tail; scheduling/write stalls mean the tail is not a proven hard one-second bound. Already committed usage remains charged, and retries cannot reverse it. A detected failure to persist pauses further training. This accepts possible undercount at the crash boundary instead of rounding up an interval the player may not have used.

When recorded usage reaches the timed budget, remaining allowance is zero. Permitted completion of the current turn and automatic settlement do not overdraw the allowance or reopen it; record any additional activity duration separately from allowance consumption. Every observed stop/continue boundary and its accounting uses the same durable writer as gameplay.

### Trusted deadlines and connectivity

Use a successful authenticated server response to establish a UTC/elapsed-time anchor bound to installation, account, protocol and immutable access/grant deadlines. Record elapsed readings at request and response; include response-delay uncertainty conservatively when deciding whether a deadline may have passed. Never extend a deadline using a delayed response or the displayed clock. Use native sleep-inclusive elapsed time and qualified continuity evidence as detailed in [trusted-device-time research](../research/trusted-device-time.md). Android combines elapsed time with boot count; the proposed iOS adapter combines `mach_continuous_time` with an app-group semaphore marker that is created before its associated anchor commits. Offline recovery only opens the stored marker; it never recreates a missing one to rehabilitate an old anchor.

Any ambiguous boot/installation/key/clock evidence, incompatible anchor version or unqualified drift behavior requires online revalidation of time-limited rights. A server time anchor alone does not verify a subscription or issue a grant. Subscription verification supplies its recognized state and original deadline; failure leaves that deadline unchanged. Do not silently replace the selected iOS path with mandatory reconnection on every process restart if native qualification fails; return for an explicit product decision.

Use app-owned stateless HTTPS nonce-echo probes on Cloudflare Workers and an AWS Lambda function URL in a region separate from the Supabase project. Neither calls Supabase. Cloudflare and Lambda use separate hosting endpoints; Lambda shares AWS as an underlying cloud with Supabase, so this is not a claim of complete provider-failure independence. Prefer the Cloudflare custom-domain endpoint and the direct Lambda HTTPS hostname, without routing both through one proxy.

Probes send only a fresh random nonce and protocol marker in a small POST body, with no account/token/customer data. Disable caching and redirects and require valid TLS, status, content type, bounded body and the matching nonce. A captive portal response, throttle, bad certificate, timeout or wrong body is unsuccessful evidence, not proof of universal internet loss. Provider network metadata may still be processed; omit payloads and credentials from application logs.

Starting/resuming a nonselected free game still requires successful account authorization. While that authorization remains valid for the foreground activity, obtain a fresh probe observation every 30 seconds; use a five-second timeout and the other endpoint on failure. Two unsuccessful endpoint checks trigger uncertainty immediately. Evidence also expires 60 seconds after the last success, covering a stalled or missed check. A network-path change invalidates evidence for the prior path and gates new interactive turns while checking the new path. A successful check preserves the same ongoing activity only if it has not already entered the stopping state. No-network observations, unsuccessful validation or expired evidence apply the accepted stopping rules and end foreground online authorization. A successful fallback establishes the needed observation even during a Supabase outage; it does not authorize a new/resumed activity or prove that Supabase is reachable. User pause, background and restart also end foreground online authorization. Ordinary cached offline rights, permitted settlement and started challenges retain their existing exceptions.

Cloudflare Workers Paid starts at $5/month, leaving roughly $20 of the target after the Supabase $25 base; Lambda requests/compute and other usage still need measurement. For scale only, 1 million Lambda requests at 128 MiB and an assumed 100 ms each cost about $0.41 before free allowances, excluding networking/logging. This is arithmetic, not a workload forecast. [Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/), [Lambda pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/lambda/pricing/), [Function URLs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lambda/latest/dg/urls-invocation.html), [Supabase regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions).

### Delivery, retries, operations and handoffs

Upload promptly after local save and reconcile on foreground entry and reconnection. Private Realtime notifications carry only an account-change hint; authorized cursor fetches supply data. Refresh subscriptions when credentials change, close them on sign-out, and check the local account-session generation before processing callbacks. Notifications are never the sole record of a change. Reconcile every 60 seconds while foregrounded and connected, coalescing with pending work. Background delivery is best effort and must use the same account writer and dependency ordering.

Retry transient failures with jittered exponential backoff, initially around one second and capped at five minutes; respect server retry-after responses. After repeated failure show pending/error state and offer a deliberate retry. Network/foreground events can prompt another bounded attempt. Authentication errors request renewal/sign-in without erasing local data; validation conflicts require review; protocol incompatibility requires update. A failed independent record does not block unrelated records, but prerequisite barriers cannot be bypassed.

Version API operations and payload encodings, keep migrations in source control, and test server/client compatibility before release. Store structured error codes and operational metrics in the accepted PostHog setup without private record bodies, tokens or raw clock anchors. Monitor failed receipts/transactions, queue age, reconciliation failures, storage growth, probe availability and backend costs. Use provider budget controls plus alerts; a budget alert does not authorize deleting history or weakening transaction/recovery behavior. Throttle optional bulk history downloads first if needed, and propose a budget/scope change before the target becomes untenable.

Use one production Supabase project and local development initially. Choose its exact region together with launch markets/data-residency requirements in release-readiness planning. Do not buy PITR or claim the provisional disaster targets are met. Carry recovery into a focused decision covering backups/independent copies, protected control state, restore testing, stale-device replay and costs. Authentication/account lifecycle owns sign-in methods, revocation, export/deletion and stale-account rejection. Subscription verification owns store integration, purchase linkage and normalized entitlement updates. Both consume stable account IDs and these authorized mutation/receipt boundaries; neither may reassign private data, purchases or allowance by editing a client owner field.

[Backend operating envelope and disaster recovery](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/28) will settle launch workload assumptions, measured storage/API costs, backup retention, recovery objectives and recovery testing within or explicitly revising the budget. [Launch markets and data residency](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/27) will settle target markets and the resulting Supabase/probe region requirements. Both remain part of the MVP map; deferral from this ticket does not mean implementation or release may ignore them.

### Required scenario acceptance

These are implementation checks, not tests claimed to have run during planning.

| Scenario | Required result |
| --- | --- |
| Kill before/after transition commit or its acknowledgement | Same committed sequence/randomness on recovery; one charge, settlement effect and receipt; reveal only committed outcomes. |
| Kill after payout but before strategy adjustment | Payout stays applied once; pending adjustment resumes or remains paused under the original rules. |
| Finish, lose response, and upload again | One final result and contribution identity; no repeated payout or allowance effect. |
| Full disk, locked key, malformed checkpoint or failed migration | Pause unsafe progression; preserve original data; recover valid state or retain incomplete history without invented results. |
| Sign out A, sign in B, then receive A's delayed response | No display, upload, grant or database mutation under B; A's unsynced records remain isolated. |
| Reinstall or restore files after allowance transfer/replacement | No recovered installation identity or duplicated spendable grant; online ownership reconciliation required. |
| A accepts R2, B submits from R1, C later edits/acknowledges | R2 remains the conflict baseline; all competing proposals retained; casino totals count it once. |
| New proposal arrives during conflict resolution | Reject stale resolution for renewed review; no unseen proposal silently discarded. |
| Delete accepted, then delayed offline edit/retry arrives | Item remains absent from totals; explicit recovery proposal only. |
| Sync page commits but acknowledgement is lost; sequence transactions contend | Cursor retry is safe; no lower-sequence late commit is skipped. |
| New-device summary bootstrap overlaps edits/deletion | Summary/totals watermark remains coherent; later feed converges; details use matching revisions. |
| A withdraws offline, B contributes, then A reconnects | Covered B contribution becomes unavailable publicly before removal is confirmed; private history remains. |
| Re-enable sharing, then retry old withdrawal or old-period result | Receipt retry does not revoke a new period; stale result cannot become public under it. |
| Source freezes transfer and target accepts but source loses response | Source cannot resume spending; target receives only the reconciled remainder once. |
| Lost-device replacement while source remains offline | Source's current grant retains original expiry; replacement waits until next eligible reset for a fresh grant. |
| Midnight/timezone change with pending final settlement | Existing contracts settle into original grant/session; no new-day refill from winnings and no duplicate reset. |
| Returning device missed policy notice | Previous policy retained for current allowance day; next eligible reset applies one new version. |
| Abrupt kill during timed thinking/animation | Apply the explicitly accepted crash-tail rule; never count downtime until relaunch or reverse committed usage. |
