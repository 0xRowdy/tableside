# Community content rights and author deletion — accepted decision

Accepted through a live Q1–Q6 discussion grounded in DMCA safe-harbor requirements and comparable remix-platform licensing precedent. This file preserves the reviewed decision for repository readers; the [resolution comment](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/29) is canonical.

Decision ticket: [Community content rights and author deletion](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/29), part of the [MVP decision map](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/1).

This is planning for the iOS/Android MVP, not final legal terms or implemented takedown tooling. Preserves the accepted [Community publication, rankings, and results](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/6) and [Authentication and account lifecycle](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/22#issuecomment-5565609337) baselines, and the [Launch markets and data residency](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/27#issuecomment-5573339375) US-only, English-only scope. Built-in/reference content licensing and the broader age/content-policy release review remain separate.

## Accepted choices

- **Q1 — License model:** publication grants True Count and other users a Terms-of-Service license, not a named open license such as CC BY-SA. A formal copyleft license would make attribution a license term downstream users could insist on, which conflicts with the already-accepted "Deleted author" anonymization on account deletion. A ToS grant keeps attribution behavior a product policy True Count controls.
- **Q2 — Grant scope:** the license covers use inside the True Count app only — execute, display, save, and remix within the product. It grants no rights to export or redistribute a strategy's instructions or prose outside the app, matching the accepted no-browser-gameplay-UI-at-launch boundary.
- **Q3 — Break-glass removal:** True Count reserves a narrow, explicit exception to the default retention rule (which otherwise always preserves other users' saved executable copies). On a validated legal complaint — e.g., substantiated copyright infringement of protected expression, not the underlying strategy or counting-method idea, which is generally not copyrightable — True Count may remove or disable an executable copy or remix even from other users' saved libraries. This is an affirmative, disclosed removal event, never a silent rewrite of someone's saved history, and propagates the same way other server-driven removals already do: marked server-side, applied by clients on next sync or reconnect.
- **Q4 — Voluntary removal versus account deletion:** these remain distinct. An author voluntarily un-publishing a strategy while keeping their account only delists it from public browse/search; their real name and attribution stay intact on other users' existing copies and remixes. "Deleted author" anonymization is reserved strictly for account deletion, per the accepted auth decision.
- **Q5 — Terms acceptance and retroactivity:** publication terms are accepted once, surfaced at first publish (reusing the existing first-publish community-profile gate), not re-shown per revision. A later material change to the terms requires re-acceptance before the player's next new publish. Already-published revisions stay governed by whichever terms version was in effect when they were published and are never retroactively rewritten, matching the accepted "don't silently rewrite historical results" principle.
- **Q6 — DMCA safe harbor:** register a designated agent with the US Copyright Office and adopt an enforced notice/counter-notice and repeat-infringer termination policy, required as a pre-launch task rather than optional. Enforcement reuses the account-standing/suspension mechanics from the already-accepted community moderation decision rather than new machinery; the existing reporting system remains the intake path and can escalate into a formal DMCA notice. Tracked separately so it doesn't block further MVP product decisions: [DMCA designated agent registration and repeat-infringer policy](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/40).

## Evidence

Checked 2026-09-07 against primary and secondary sources.

- [17 U.S.C. § 512 full text (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512) and the [Copyright Office § 512 resource page](https://www.copyright.gov/512/): § 512(c) safe harbor for content stored at a user's direction requires a registered designated agent, a compliant notice-and-takedown/counter-notification flow (10–14 business days to restore after an uncontested valid counter-notice), and — as a threshold condition for any § 512 safe harbor, not just § 512(c) — an actually-enforced repeat-infringer termination policy. Safe harbor is a liability shield, not a licensing scheme.
- [DMCA Designated Agent Directory](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/) and its [FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/faq.html): online-only registration, renews every 3 years; the $6 fee is consistent across secondary sources but was not independently confirmed on a primary fee-schedule document.
- [GitHub Terms of Service](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service): a fork's grant to GitHub is the forker's own, so forks survive the original repository's removal; GitHub states it will not delete content contributed to other users' repositories or forked by other users. This is the precedent for why a remix's continued existence should not depend on the original staying licensed.
- Scratch's CC BY-SA-based remix licensing was described via secondary sources (Scratch Wiki, community discussion) after the live terms page returned a 404 during research; treat the Scratch specifics as unverified against its current terms, included only as a second data point for the "independent grant" pattern, not relied on for True Count's own license choice (True Count uses a ToS grant, per Q1, not a copyleft license).
